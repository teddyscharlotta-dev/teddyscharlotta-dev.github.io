Cold-Storage Shelving for Prepreg Inventory

Bottom line Designed and fabricated welded steel racking that converted a single-level prepreg freezer into full-height storage, sized to the largest box in inventory and rated for roughly 100 lb per box. Modeled in SketchUp, welded by a two-person team, and in daily production use.

The problem

Prepreg has to be held below freezing to keep the resin from advancing, so the shop's inventory lives in a dedicated freezer unit. That freezer had no internal structure — boxes were stacked directly on the floor of the cabinet, which wasted most of the vertical volume, made anything but the top box inaccessible, and put the weight of a stack on the material at the bottom. The shop needed the same footprint to hold substantially more material and to make every box reachable without unstacking.

Constraints

  • Envelope. The unit had to fill the freezer interior without blocking the door swing or the evaporator, so I measured the usable interior rather than working from the cabinet's nominal dimensions.
  • Shelf pitch. Level spacing was set by the largest prepreg box in inventory plus the clearance needed to slide it in and out, which fixed how many levels the height would allow.
  • Load. Each level had to carry roughly 100 lb boxes without deflecting enough to bind the box above it.
  • Environment. A freezer that gets opened repeatedly cycles through the dew point, so the structure sees condensation and frost. Corrosion resistance and weldability mattered more than minimum weight.

What I did

I measured the freezer interior and the largest box in inventory, then modeled several shelving configurations in SketchUp to trade level count against clearance — more levels store more material, but only until the pitch gets too tight to remove a box cleanly. The model made that trade visible before any steel was cut, and settled how many levels the cabinet would actually support.

For structure I specified 1.5 in square steel tube. At the spans involved it carried the load with margin, it welds cleanly, and the closed section gives far less surface for frost to collect on than an open channel or angle would. I then fabricated the unit with one other team member, welding the frame and levels to the modeled dimensions.

What I learned

This was the first project where I owned the whole path from measurement to installed hardware, and the lesson was how much the fabrication constraints should have informed the model earlier. A few joints that were trivial to draw in SketchUp were awkward to reach with a torch once the frame was tacked together, and the sequence we welded in ended up mattering as much as the geometry. On the next one I'd sketch the weld sequence alongside the model rather than treating fabrication as something that happens after the design is finished.

Skills practiced

  • Dimensional measurement and design to a fixed envelope
  • SketchUp solid modeling and design iteration
  • Structural material selection for load and service environment
  • Steel fabrication and welding in a two-person team
  • Translating a workflow problem into a hardware requirement
