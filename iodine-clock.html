Iodine Clock Calibration for a Chem-E-Car Stopping Mechanism

Bottom line Ran and reduced more than 200 timed iodine clock trials across five ascorbic acid concentrations, producing a linear concentration–time calibration (t = 937 · C − 29.4, R² = 0.98) that lets the team set a target stopping time by reagent mass alone. Tracking down the sources of scatter along the way cut run-to-run variation at the competition concentration from 8.7% to 3.0% CV.

The problem

A Chem-E-Car has to travel a distance announced only an hour before competition, and it has to stop there without a driver, a timer, or any electronic control of the stopping event. Our team uses an iodine clock reaction as the trigger: two solutions are combined, the mixture stays clear for a predictable interval, and the sudden appearance of the starch–iodine complex is what cuts the motor. The engineering problem is therefore not chemistry for its own sake. It is reproducibility. A reaction that fires at the right average time but scatters by fifteen percent will miss the target distance.

What I did

I ran the reaction testing with one other team member. We prepared a fixed two-beaker protocol, with ascorbic acid and iodine tincture in 30 mL of distilled water on one side and hydrogen peroxide and starch indicator on the other, and held every volume constant so that ascorbic acid concentration was the only variable. We then timed the interval to color change across 0.05, 0.10, 0.125, 0.15, and 0.20 M, running fifteen to thirty replicates at each point over two seasons.

Data analysis

Reaction time rises close to linearly with ascorbic acid concentration across this range. A least-squares fit to the five concentration means gives t = 937 C − 29.4 (R² = 0.98) for the current season and t = 771 C − 8.0 (R² = 0.98) for the prior one, so a target stop time can be interpolated from reagent mass instead of tested from scratch for every new distance.

The scatter carried more information than the fit. The 0.05 M runs came back at 86% coefficient of variation, and three of those thirty trials read 92 to 101 seconds against a group otherwise clustered near 19, all logged on the day the team switched iodine tincture lots. Excluding those three on that documented cause, and no others from either season, leaves a mean of 19.5 s at 15% CV. The variance was thus traced to procedure rather than chemistry. Controlling those brought the 0.125 M condition, the one closest to competition, from 8.7% to 3.0% CV.
